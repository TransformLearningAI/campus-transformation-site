01
The Moment

Hampshire College closes after fall 2026. Fifty-five years of self-directed, interdisciplinary, project-based education — the school that produced Ken Burns, Jon Krakauer, Eugene Mirman, and thousands of people who learned to think for themselves — ends not because the idea failed, but because the financial model did.

The idea was never the problem. It produced graduates who start companies, make films, write books, build movements, and solve problems that traditional graduates don't even see. Hampshire proved that you don't need grades, majors, or departments to educate extraordinary people. You need a question, a community, and the freedom to pursue both.

"This is an incalculable loss, the reverberations of which will be felt in ways none of us can imagine."
— Ken Burns, Hampshire alumnus

That idea doesn't require a campus. It doesn't require accreditation. It doesn't require a $60 million endowment. It requires people — and those people are still here. Whether it can work in a new form is an open question. This document is a starting point for that experiment, not a finished blueprint. Many of the ideas here are untested. Some will fail. That's the point — Hampshire always valued the inquiry over the answer.

02
What Is Hampshire Forward?

Hampshire Forward is a learning cooperative — a member-governed, distributed organization that carries Hampshire's pedagogical DNA into a new form. It is not a college. It does not grant degrees. It is not trying to replace Hampshire College or compete with Hampshire Next's effort to preserve the campus.

It starts with a question, not an answer: What if the best parts of Hampshire — the Division system, narrative evaluation, self-directed inquiry, interdisciplinary collaboration, and engagement with the world's real problems — didn't need a campus to survive? We don't know yet. But we think it's worth finding out — together, in public, iterating as we go.

Hampshire Next is saving the campus. Hampshire Forward is carrying the mission. They complement each other.

Who it serves:

• Former Hampshire faculty who just lost their jobs and want to keep teaching
• Former Hampshire staff who built the institution and have skills the world needs
• Hampshire alumni of every decade who want to keep learning the way they learned best
• Current and recent students whose education was interrupted
• Anyone who wants access to Hampshire-style learning but never had the chance to attend

Legal structure: Cooperative nonprofit (501(c)(3)) — tax-deductible donations, grant-eligible, democratically governed. One member, one vote.

03
Three Divisions

Hampshire organized learning into three progressive divisions. Hampshire Forward proposes adapting that structure — but these are starting hypotheses, not fixed plans. Which formats work in a distributed model? What price points are sustainable? How do narrative evaluations function without the intimacy of a small campus? These are research questions we'll answer by doing, testing, and adjusting. The first year is explicitly experimental.

Division I

Learning Collaboratives

Short-term (4-8 week) interdisciplinary learning experiences led by former Hampshire faculty, alumni experts, and guest practitioners. No grades. Narrative feedback. Project-based. Sliding scale: $200-$800. Open questions: What's the right length? Does virtual delivery preserve the magic? What cohort size works best? We'll run the first 3-4 collaboratives as explicit pilots and gather data before scaling.

Division II

Applied Research & Social Action

Semester-length (12-16 week) team-based projects addressing real social, environmental, or community problems. Teams partner with real organizations. Deliverables are public: reports, tools, films, campaigns, curricula. Quarterly showcases.

Division III

The Fellows Program

Year-long independent projects for serious practitioners at any stage of life. 10-15 fellows per year receive stipends ($5,000-$15,000), mentorship, and network access. This is the most ambitious and least tested part of the model. Can self-directed year-long work happen without a campus community? The first cohort will help us answer that.

07
Action Priorities

A phased plan for turning this idea into something real. Each phase builds on the last — nothing launches until the foundation beneath it is solid.

Phase 1 — Immediate

Establish the Foundation

Online presence. Contact list. Mission statement. Volunteer leaders. Before anything else, we need a nucleus — a small group of committed people, a clear articulation of what this is, and a way for the community to find us and raise their hand.

Phase 2 — Months 1–3

Activate the Network

Alumni-to-alumni mentoring network connecting new grads to older alums. Virtual events — panels, conversations, workshops. Oral history collection: recording Hampshire stories before they scatter. This phase is about turning a contact list into a living community.

Phase 3 — Months 3–6

Formalize the Organization

Membership launch. Grant applications. 501(c)(3) incorporation. Local partnerships with institutions, nonprofits, and community organizations. This is where Hampshire Forward becomes a legal entity with real money and real obligations.

Phase 4 — Months 6–12

The Hampshire Archive

Becoming the curator of Hampshire's history. Building a digital archive first — syllabi, student work, photographs, oral histories, institutional records. Finding physical space. Hiring an archivist. If Hampshire College ceases to exist, someone needs to ensure its story doesn't disappear with it.

Phase 5 — Year 2+

Long-Term Stewardship

Annual gathering. Exhibits. Publications. Long-term stewardship of Hampshire's legacy and the cooperative's mission. By this point, Hampshire Forward isn't just preserving the past — it's building something new on the foundation of what Hampshire proved was possible.

Revenue Structures

These are proposed revenue channels. Which ones actually work is something we'll discover by testing. Some will generate real revenue. Some will need to be rethought or abandoned. The cooperative structure means members help decide which experiments to run and which to retire.

Stream 1

Paid Learning Collaboratives

4-8 week courses, sliding scale $200-$800. The core product.

Stream 2

Certificate Programs

Stackable certificates: AI & Ethics, Documentary & Social Change, Community-Based Research, Environmental Justice Leadership. 3 collaboratives + capstone = certificate. $1,500-$3,000.

Stream 3

Contracted Experiences for Schools

The Hampshire Experience: 1-2 day self-directed learning workshops for high schools, community colleges, and underserved youth. $2,000-$5,000 per workshop. Untested — needs pilots with 3-5 schools before scaling.

Stream 4

Corporate & Organizational Learning

Hampshire-style innovation workshops for companies and nonprofits. $5,000-$15,000 per engagement.

Stream 5

Named Fellowships

$15,000/year per named fellowship. Target: 20 within 3 years = $300K annual recurring.

Integration with Existing Institutions

Hampshire Forward doesn't have to exist in isolation. Its experiences can overlap with, integrate into, or substitute for classes at colleges, community colleges, and vocational-technical schools. Here's how — and honestly, which of these models actually work is something we'll need to test with real institutional partners.

For Four-Year Colleges
Overlap

Interdisciplinary Electives

A Hampshire Forward collaborative on "AI Ethics Through Documentary Film" or "Climate Migration and Community Design" could serve as an elective at a partner college. Students get exposure to Hampshire-style inquiry within their existing degree program. The college gets an innovative offering without hiring new faculty. Hampshire Forward gets tuition-share revenue and institutional credibility. This needs willing registrars and faculty champions at partner schools. We'll start by approaching Five College institutions and progressive schools like Goddard, Prescott, and Bennington.

Substitute

Senior Capstone Alternative

Hampshire's Division III was a year-long independent project — the most transformative part of the Hampshire experience. Hampshire Forward's Fellows Program could serve as a capstone alternative for seniors at partner colleges who want a self-directed final project with real-world impact instead of a conventional thesis. The student enrolls through their home institution, works with a Hampshire Forward advisor, and produces a public-facing project. The home school grants the credit; we provide the mentorship and methodology.

Integrate

January Term & Summer Intensives

Many colleges have J-term or summer sessions where students take short, intensive courses. Hampshire Forward collaboratives fit perfectly into these windows — 4-week immersive experiences in project-based, interdisciplinary inquiry. Colleges contract with Hampshire Forward as a provider, students get credit, and faculty get a break or co-teach. This is potentially the easiest entry point because J-term and summer programming is always hard for schools to fill.

For Community Colleges
Overlap

Honors Enrichment

Community college honors programs are often underfunded and understaffed. Hampshire Forward collaboratives could serve as honors enrichment experiences — students in the honors program take a Hampshire Forward collaborative alongside their regular coursework. It introduces self-directed, interdisciplinary thinking to students who are academically strong but have never encountered this kind of learning. Sliding scale pricing or grant-funded to ensure access.

Substitute

General Education Alternative

A Hampshire Forward collaborative in "Community-Based Research Methods" or "Environmental Justice Leadership" could substitute for a gen-ed humanities or social science requirement at a partner community college. Instead of a lecture-based intro course, students do real project work with narrative evaluation. This requires articulation agreements with specific community colleges — a slow process, but one that could fundamentally change what gen-ed looks like for students who learn best by doing.

Integrate

Transfer Bridge Program

Community college students planning to transfer to four-year schools often struggle with the transition to independent, self-directed academic work. A Hampshire Forward collaborative taken during their final community college semester serves as a bridge experience — they practice self-directed inquiry, build a portfolio with narrative evaluations, and arrive at their transfer institution with skills most freshmen don't develop until junior year.

Access

Weekend & Evening Workshops

Many community college students work full-time. Hampshire Forward can offer condensed weekend workshops (Friday evening through Sunday) at community college campuses — intensive experiences in design thinking, interdisciplinary problem-solving, or AI literacy. The community college provides the space; Hampshire Forward provides the faculty and curriculum. Students who can't commit to a full collaborative get a meaningful taste. Untested format — we need to pilot with 2-3 community colleges to see if weekend intensives attract working students or if the timing still doesn't work.

For Vocational-Technical Schools
Overlap

Humanities + Trades Integration

Vocational-technical students learn welding, electrical, HVAC, healthcare — but rarely get exposure to critical thinking, ethics, communication, or interdisciplinary problem-solving. Hampshire Forward can offer integrated modules that connect the trades to broader questions: "The Ethics of Building" (construction + philosophy), "Health Systems and Community" (healthcare trades + social science), "Energy, Climate, and the Work We Do" (HVAC/electrical + environmental studies). These aren't replacements for technical training — they're the missing humanities layer. Vo-tech programs are pragmatic. We need to demonstrate that these modules improve job readiness and communication skills, not just add more coursework.

Substitute

Applied Project Capstone

Many vo-tech programs require a final project or portfolio. Hampshire Forward's Division II model — a semester-long project addressing a real community problem — could serve as that capstone. An HVAC student partners with a community organization to audit and redesign the heating system in a local nonprofit's building. A healthcare student researches health access barriers in their county and presents findings to the local health department. The trade skill meets the real world.

Integrate

Entrepreneurship for Tradespeople

Most vo-tech graduates will work for someone else. Some will start their own businesses. Hampshire Forward can offer entrepreneurship collaboratives specifically for trades students — business planning, client communication, marketing, financial literacy, and AI tools for small business. Not a generic business course, but one designed around the realities of starting an electrical company or a welding shop. College of St. Joseph the Worker in Ohio is already blending liberal arts with trades — we can learn from their model while adding Hampshire's project-based, self-directed approach.

The Contractor Model

In all of these scenarios, Hampshire Forward operates as a contractor — an external provider delivering a specific educational experience that the host institution can't or doesn't offer on its own. This is how it works:

ElementHampshire Forward ProvidesHost Institution Provides
FacultyTrained facilitators with Hampshire pedagogySpace (if in-person), students, credit approval
CurriculumCollaborative design, project frameworks, evaluation rubricsIntegration into degree/program requirements
AssessmentNarrative evaluations, portfolio reviewCredit translation, transcript entry
Cost$2,000-$8,000 per contracted experienceFunded through existing program budgets, grants, or student fees

Why institutions would do this: They get innovative programming without hiring new faculty, curriculum development, or accreditation changes. They can test interdisciplinary, project-based learning in a contained way — one course, one cohort, one semester — before committing to structural change. And they can tell their accreditors, their boards, and their prospective students that they're partnering with an organization rooted in 55 years of proven alternative pedagogy.

Why we'd do this: It generates revenue, expands reach, tests our model in diverse institutional contexts, and — most importantly — puts Hampshire-style education in front of students who would never have had access to it. A first-generation community college student in rural Ohio doing a Hampshire Forward collaborative is the whole point.

All of these integration models are hypotheses. We don't know which institutional partners will say yes, which formats will work for which student populations, or whether the contractor model generates enough revenue to be sustainable. We need to approach 10-15 institutions, pilot 3-5 partnerships, and learn from what happens before we claim any of this works at scale.

Open Questions

Hampshire was founded as an experiment. Hampshire Forward is one too. Here are the things we don't know yet.

Pedagogy

Can Hampshire's model survive without a campus?

Does the magic transfer to a distributed model? What gets lost? What might get better? We need honest data, not cheerleading.

Community

Can you build real intellectual community online?

Hampshire's late-night dorm conversations can't be replicated on Zoom. What can? Regional gatherings? Retreats? Hybrid models? We genuinely don't know.

Economics

Will people pay for non-credentialed learning?

Maybe. Maybe not at the price points we're proposing. We need to test and be willing to restructure.

AI

Can AI enhance narrative evaluation — or does it cheapen it?

This needs careful testing with real faculty and real participants before we build a system around it.

Access

Can a 1-2 day workshop change how underserved students see learning?

We need to pilot with a handful of schools, gather real feedback, and be honest about what a short experience can and can't do.

Governance

Can a cooperative make decisions fast enough to survive?

Democratic governance is a value. It's also slow. Can we balance member voice with startup agility?

Identity

Is this for Hampshire people, or for everyone?

The alumni community is the launch base, but long-term sustainability requires reaching beyond it. This tension needs to be navigated carefully.

This is not a business plan pretending to have all the answers. It's an invitation to brainstorm, test, research, iterate, and build — the same way Hampshire always worked.
